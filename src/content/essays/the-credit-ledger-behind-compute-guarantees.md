Nvidia's [November 12 announcement](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html) that co-founder Jensen Huang is stepping down immediately, with CFO Colette Kress named interim chief executive and Harvey Jones appointed board chair, triggered a roughly 12 percent drop in Nvidia shares ahead of fiscal third-quarter earnings scheduled for November 17. Management and the board stated that customer commitments and product roadmaps will continue without interruption. Under a single-class voting structure, the transition still shifts investor scrutiny directly onto Kress's existing domain, the balance sheet commitments and customer credit backstops disclosed outside the income statement.

Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter [Form 10-Q](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf), published August 26, 2026 for the period ended July 26, shows why quarterly revenue figures leave out the main risk distribution. Alongside $96.2 billion in quarterly revenue, Note 10 discloses $366 billion in conventional future commitments, including $279 billion for supply and capacity, up $160 billion or 134.5 percent from $119 billion the prior quarter. Beyond ordinary supply contracts, the filing lists $36 billion in AI cloud agreements, $20 billion in data-center leases intended for third-party transfer, and $108.5 billion in maximum guarantee exposure after August subsequent events.

Those off-income-statement obligations turn an equipment vendor into a credit backstop. Up to $105 billion of the guarantees back construction by SB Energy affiliates at the roughly 4.25-gigawatt PORTS campus in Pike County, Ohio for OpenAI Group PBC affiliates, taking effect across nine phases beginning in fiscal 2029 on 20-year leases in exchange for exclusive Nvidia hardware deployment. Customer concentration reinforces the link. Three direct customers contributed 44 percent of Nvidia's half-year revenue (16, 15, and 13 percent), and five accounted for 70 percent of period-end receivables. Outside the 10-Q, Nvidia invested $2 billion in CoreWeave equity at $87.20 per share in January, while CoreWeave carried $35.068 billion in recourse and non-recourse debt on June 30 and closed a $2.6 billion five-year loan facility in August backed by customer contracts averaging three years, a financing chain documented in [Eqvero's Nvidia supply-commitments case](https://eqvero.com/cases/nvidia-customer-concentration-and-supply-commitments).

Hardware profits and credit guarantees clear on different clocks. Equipment revenue is recognized when clusters ship, while lease backstops, cloud-capacity floors, and debt-renewal exposure persist for years. In Jensen and Meckling's 1976 agency framework, outside shareholders bear monitoring costs whenever management allocates capital across multi-party claims where upside and downside diverge. If AI cloud demand stays tight, partners can redirect capacity under the $36 billion agreements to higher-paying third parties and Nvidia recovers its commitments. If customer financing tightens or campus utilization lags, procurement lock-ins, receivable collections, and contingent guarantees come under pressure in the same cycle.
