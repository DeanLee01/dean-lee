Anthropic filed its amended registration statement on Tuesday, setting terms for an initial public offering that will test the liquidity of global equity markets. The company plans to sell 825 million shares at a price range of $70 to $78 apiece, implying a market valuation of roughly $1.33 trillion. At the $74 midpoint, the offering will raise more than $61 billion in gross proceeds, with shares scheduled to begin trading Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. It represents the largest equity financing event in the history of the technology industry, shifting the company from private secondary marks to daily public price discovery.

The valuation establishes an aggressive benchmark. In its initial Form S-1 filing two weeks earlier, Anthropic reported first-half revenue of $16.3 billion, putting its current run rate between $32 billion and $35 billion annually. At $1.33 trillion, the public market is asked to pay approximately 38 to 40 times annualized revenue. High revenue multiples are familiar in enterprise software cycles, though traditional software businesses earned those multiples by combining 80 percent gross margins with minimal balance sheet commitments. Anthropic operates under a distinct cost structure.

Alongside its revenue growth, the company disclosed $518 billion in multi-year infrastructure commitments spanning the next decade. Roughly 80 percent of those contracts are take-or-pay obligations, requiring fixed cash outlays for power, data center leases, and accelerator hardware regardless of actual token consumption. That total includes $111 billion committed to Alphabet, $110 billion to Amazon, and $161 billion in specialized equipment lease structures tied to Broadcom silicon. In economic terms, these obligations function like senior debt service, sitting ahead of common shareholders in the queue for operating cash flow.

This combination of a high sales multiple and heavy fixed liabilities alters the risk distribution. When a firm carries large fixed operating commitments, common equity behaves like an out-of-the-money call option. If enterprise inference demand compounds and pricing power holds, incremental revenues drop directly to the bottom line, delivering substantial cash flow after fixed obligations are met. If model distillation, open weights, or multi-model routing compress token prices, those fixed infrastructure payments do not decline. In that scenario, falling revenue compresses margins against rigid fixed costs, turning operating cash flow negative.

The sheer size of the float introduces a separate market clearing challenge. Absorbing $61 billion of primary equity in a single transaction requires substantial participation from institutional balance sheets, including sovereign wealth funds, global mutual funds, and passive managers anticipating benchmark inclusion. That capital must be reallocated from existing holdings, creating a liquidity drain across the broader technology equity complex as portfolio managers make room for a new mega-cap allocation.

The primary defense for this valuation is balance sheet resilience. Net proceeds of roughly $60 billion provide Anthropic with an enormous cash buffer. That capital gives the firm the ability to fulfill its multi-year compute contracts without returning to volatile private credit desks or dependent venture rounds. It also insulates the company against the bank loan syndication friction that has recently slowed third-party data center developers.

Investors purchasing shares on Thursday are pricing an asymmetric distribution. Sustained high-margin enterprise adoption drives the upside, while half a trillion dollars of contractual hardware debt defines the downside risk. At $1.33 trillion, the clearing hurdle is set, and the market will begin pricing that distribution immediately.
